# Version 6 — Instant Replay / Clips

## Main Goal

Save recent gameplay after an event occurs using a bounded rolling encoded buffer. Reuse V5 capture, V3 audio and V4 visibility policy. Instant replay is opt-in: an idle installed mod must not continuously encode when replay is disabled.

## User Experience

Enable Instant Replay once, choose 15 seconds, 30 seconds, 60 seconds, 2 minutes or 5 minutes, and press the configurable F10 binding to save a clip. Feedback explains when the buffer is still warming and how much history is available. Saving a clip during a full recording does not interrupt that recording or start another capture pipeline.

## New Features

- A rolling encoded history with configurable duration and an explicit byte/storage budget.
- Save Clip from a keybind or secondary action, with non-intrusive progress and completion feedback.
- Shared capture/encode operation for replay and full recording when their resolved settings match.
- A truthful state model: disabled, warming, ready, saving and unavailable/failed.

## Technical Work

Evaluate encoded-packet storage against closed, short temporary segments using the actual V5 backends. Prefer the simpler reliable option if gains are small. Keep timestamps, keyframe locations, codec configuration and all enabled audio tracks together. Never buffer minutes of raw RGBA frames or lossless defaults.

For a process-based encoder, investigate a segment/mux output arrangement before building a packet parser. Each backend should expose a bounded encoded-history contract rather than leaking FFmpeg process assumptions into UI. Add explicit reference ownership so a clip export can pin segments while the ring continues evicting unpinned history.

Clip boundaries must be decodable: start at an available keyframe, or use a validated boundary-trim method. Explain any duration rounding; do not cut arbitrary packet bytes. Rebase video/audio timestamps together and preserve track labels, offsets and clean-capture policy. Recording start while replay is armed should reuse the live stream where safe; incompatible requested settings need a clear transition policy rather than an automatic second encoder.

Define a global disk/memory budget including ring, pinned exports and full recordings. Queue clip requests with a small limit; coalesce identical pending requests or refuse excess requests visibly. Settings changes that restart encoding reset replay history with notice.

## Performance Work

Replay encoding has a real continuous cost and must be disclosed. Saving a clip should copy/remux already-encoded data off-thread without an extra render/readback/encode pass. Track ring bytes, encoder bitrate, pinned bytes, eviction time and clip export disk throughput.

Provisional targets: replay-disabled performance within V5 measurement noise; armed replay within 5% p95/p99 frame-time and 3% 1% low difference from an equivalent full-recording pipeline. Clip-save acknowledgment within 100 ms; bounded memory/disk use even under repeated requests. Do not promise a fixed export completion time independent of clip size and storage speed. Admission must reserve space before pinning data so export cannot exhaust all ring capacity.

Re-run matched OBS comparisons where comparable replay behavior exists; compare ordinary full recording separately. Verify unique-frame delivery rather than reporting only a nominal 60/120 FPS container rate.

## UI / UX

Keep replay separate from the normal Record button. A small ready/warming indicator is optional. Show duration choices and estimated storage based on measured bitrate, including uncertainty. F10 must be configurable and checked for binding conflicts. Reuse V2 completion feedback for clips.

## Reliability

Handle full disk, interrupted export, crash, bitrate spikes, encoder restart and shutdown with pinned data. Promote completed files atomically; preserve source history until success when budgets permit. A failed clip export must not stop a healthy full recording unless both are affected by a shared fatal storage failure.

Known risks include keyframe duration mismatch, audio priming offsets, segment gaps, variable bitrate exceeding estimates, and export pinning preventing eviction. Define an explicit overload order: reject new clip requests before risking the active full recording.

## Compatibility

Retain all V1–V5 supported capture/audio combinations. Validate containers and every available encoder's timestamp/keyframe behavior. On a backend that cannot share encoded output safely, mark replay unavailable or require a mode switch; do not silently launch another expensive pipeline. Flashback remains independent and optional.

## Testing Requirements

- Pass V1–V5 regressions, recovery and audio synchronization tests.
- Test every duration, warming-state saves and exact event retention near keyframe/segment boundaries.
- Save overlapping clips repeatedly during multi-hour full recordings; verify bounded storage and uninterrupted output.
- Inject disk exhaustion, crashes during pin/export/rename, encoder resets and settings changes.
- Decode exported clips and inspect first/last frames, A/V alignment, all tracks and timestamp continuity.
- Benchmark armed replay, full recording, combined use and clip export under realistic disk contention.

## Completion Criteria

Requested recent events survive in valid, synchronized clips; duration rounding is documented; budgets cannot grow without bound; and simultaneous clip saving does not add a second capture pipeline or disrupt the primary recording. Replay-disabled users incur no rolling-buffer work.

## Explicitly Not Included Yet

No permanent recording archive of all gameplay, cloud clips, sharing service, automatic highlight detection, lossless rolling default or full video editor. Marker workflows belong to V7. Do not support conflicting full/replay quality settings through duplicate encoders by default.

## Notes for the Next Version

Give V7 clip-to-session time mappings, retained history ranges and stable recording identities. A marker should be able to refer to original session time even when a clip has rebased its timeline or history has expired.
